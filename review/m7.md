## Changes

Edits from the interrupted pass, all kept:

- **m7-04**: The distractor "Sit straight up and stay still" became "Sit upright even as the rider leans into the turn". The old wording was partly right: "stay still" echoes the manual's own advice, "Limit conversation and movement when the cycle is in operation" (p.32). The new one contradicts "Remain directly behind and lean with the rider through turns and curves" (p.32) and nothing else. Key unchanged.
- **m7-06**: The distractor "Turn your head fully around so they can hear you" became "…so the passenger can hear you clearly". The key was 66 characters against at most 50 (1.32×), above the build's 1.3× threshold. It is now 66 against 65. Key unchanged. Support, p.32: "If the rider must speak with the passenger while riding, a slight head turn while maintaining focus on the road and traffic conditions ahead is suggested."
- **m7-07**: The distractor "Lowering the headlight beam" became "Raising the handlebars above shoulder height". The old distractor is standard advice in MSF-based training: the MSF-supplied Missouri Motorcycle Operator Manual tells riders carrying a passenger to use the headlight's adjusting screws to "lower the beam" ([dor.mo.gov/forms/2332.pdf](https://dor.mo.gov/forms/2332.pdf)). This manual's own requirement of a "properly aimed headlight" (p.12) also lets a learner argue for it. The new distractor is illegal under p.12: "Motorcycle handlebars must not rise above an operator's shoulders when properly seated on the motorcycle." Key unchanged. Support, p.32: "Minor adjustments to the cycle's suspension and tire pressure may be required depending on passenger weight."
- **m7-09**: The distractor "The headlight may aim too high" became "The turn signals may stop flashing", for the same reason as m7-07: loaded bags lower the rear and raise the beam. I also added the manual's instruction to the explanation: "so keep the weight in each bag about the same". Support, p.33: "make certain the load distribution in each bag is approximately the same. Failing to distribute the load evenly may cause the cycle to pull to one side." Key unchanged. See judgment call 2.
- **m7-10**: Three distractors were replaced: "Bags rubbing the paint", "Reduced fuel economy only" and "A dimmer taillight". The key was 62 characters against at most 25 (2.48×), and it was the only option that described a mechanism. The new options are parallel "Bags…" mechanisms (fuel line, throttle cable, shift lever) of 55–63 characters. The manual mentions none of them. The only overloading danger it names is on p.33: "Overloading may also cause the bags to catch in the wheel or chain, locking the rear wheel and prompting the cycle to skid." Key unchanged.
- **m7-11**: The distractor "Ride as one group with the most experienced rider last" became "…with the newest riders at the back". The old one agreed with the manual's placement advice, which puts newer riders up front, "carefully watched by more experienced riders" (p.33). Only its "one group" part was wrong. The new one contradicts both the placement advice and the split. Key unchanged. Support, p.33: "Should the number of cyclists traveling together exceed five or six, it is recommended that the riders split into two separate travel groups".
- **m7-12**: Two distractors changed.
  - "In the lead position" became "In a separate group made up of other new riders". The lead position arguably is "towards the front", and the manual never says the leader must be experienced. A group made only of new riders has no one experienced to watch them.
  - "Riding alongside an experienced rider" became "Side by side with an experienced rider who can coach them". The key had been the only option with a reason clause, and it was 57 characters against at most 37 (1.54×). The new option is still wrong: "Riders should avoid "pairing up" while traveling" (p.34).

  Key unchanged. Support, p.33: "Place inexperienced riders towards the front of the group where they can be carefully watched by more experienced riders."
- **m7-14**: Three short distractors were lengthened. The key was 70 characters against at most 35 (2.0×); the options are now 62–66. The replaced "Only in heavy rain" was also partly defensible from rider training: MSF's Guide to Group Riding calls for single file in poor visibility or road conditions ([MSF sheet](https://www.pgrny.org/images/documents/msf_group_ride_safety_sheet.pdf)). Key unchanged. Support, p.34: "Riders should break with formation and travel single file when approaching turns or curves and when entering or exiting freeways."

My own changes:

- **m7-14 explanation**: Added that the staggered formation is the norm the rest of the time, including on straight roads and on the freeway itself. The most tempting distractor, "…while cruising on freeways", shares the word "freeways" with the key, and the explanation now says why it is wrong. Support, p.34: "Group riders are encouraged to travel in a "staggered" formation."
- **m7-13 explanation**: The stem depends on the sentence the original writer found ambiguous, "The third rider would follow in behind the first rider, leaving a minimum two second travel cushion between the first rider" (p.34). The explanation now points to the "Staggering" diagram on p.33, which settles the reading: it labels "2 SECONDS" between the leader and the third rider, and between the second and fourth riders. The explanation also says the manual gives no figure for the diagonal gap to the second rider. An MSF-trained learner may remember "one second" for that gap, and the stem deliberately asks about the same-track gap. Key unchanged.
- **m7-15 explanation**: Added that the leader returns "promptly" to the original lane, and that riders keep their original lane positions once past. Support, p.34: "the lead rider should quickly pass the motor vehicle and promptly return to the original lane of travel. Once safely past, riders are encouraged to maintain their original lane positions."
- **m7-16 explanation**: Added two points.
  - The legal window starts 30 minutes after sunset, not at sunset, which says why the tempting "From sunset to sunrise" is wrong.
  - The manual's separate recommendation to ride with the headlight on at all times is not the legal rule. This matters because m4-06 teaches that recommendation as an answer.

  Support, p.35: "Massachusetts law requires riders to use their headlights from one-half (½) hour after sunset to one-half (½) hour before sunrise." p.20: "The best way to alert motor vehicle drivers of your presence on a motorcycle is to keep the headlight on at all times."
- **Summary bullet 8 (inexperienced riders)**: Changed "near the front" to "toward the front… where more experienced riders can watch them". Without the reason, "near the front" can be read as "in the lead". Support, p.33, quoted under m7-12.
- **Summary bullet 9 (staggered formation)**: Changed "never ride side by side" to "avoid pairing up side by side". The manual's word is "avoid" (p.34), and p.51 allows "Ride no more than two abreast", so "never" overstated it. I also added the formation's positions. Support, p.34: "places the lead rider to the left side of the travel lane with the second rider just behind and to the right side of the travel lane". The p.33 diagram matches.

## Checked with no change

- **Questions**: m7-01, 02, 03, 05 and 08 needed no change from either pass. m7-04, 06, 07, 10, 11 and 12 needed nothing beyond the kept edits above.
- **Summary bullets**: The other 10 bullets (1–7, 10–12) are accurate and paraphrased. Bullet 1's longest shared run is the 6-word list "handling, balance, acceleration, and stopping distance".

Specific confirmations requested in the brief:

- **Staggered-spacing sentence (p.34).** m7-13 and bullet 9 depend on reading it as "the third rider stays at least two seconds behind the first". The p.33 diagram supports that reading unambiguously. Nothing in the section uses a figure for the diagonal gap, which the manual never gives.
- **Passenger-seating sentences (p.32).** There are two candidates.
  - "Sit comfortably close to the rider without hindering rider control." Bullet 3's "sit close without interfering with control" holds under any reading of it.
  - "Riders should not have to adjust seating position to accommodate passengers." m7-07's explanation restates it without interpreting it. Its distractor "Moving the rider's seating position far forward" is wrong whether the sentence is an equipment standard or a direct instruction.

  No question asks how or where the passenger sits.
- **Group-passing diagrams (p.34).** Stage 1 shows the leader back on the left side of the lane ahead of the car. Stage 2 shows the second rider back on the right and the next rider signaling to pass. m7-15 and bullet 10 rely only on the text (one at a time, leader first), and the diagrams agree with it. The drawings can't reliably show whether the next rider moves up to the left position before passing, as MSF teaches, and nothing tests that.
- **m7-16 against current law.** MGL c.85 §15 still requires lights "during the period of 1/2 hour after sunset to 1/2 hour before sunrise" ([malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter85/Section15)). MGL c.90 §7 sets no daytime headlight rule for motorcycles ([malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter90/Section7)). So the "Under Massachusetts law" stem holds, and `volatile: false` is right.
- **m7-01.** MGL c.90 §7 requires headgear for passengers. Eye protection is required only of the operator, and only when the bike has no windshield (p.9 and §7), so "Eye protection only" is wrong on two counts. Boots (p.14) and a reflective vest (p.20) are recommendations, not law.
- **Passenger questions m7-02, 03, 04 and 06** match the p.32 list. The MSF list agrees: "Get on the motorcycle only after you have started the engine and have the transmission in neutral" (Missouri manual, above).
- **m7-11.** Eight riders exceeds both the manual's "five or six" and MSF's "ideally five to seven riders", so the key holds under either.
- **m7-13.** "Four seconds" can't be defended from p.25's four-second rule, because the stem asks for the minimum.
- **Overlap with m2.** m2-05 tests the same passenger-helmet fact as m7-01. If both land in one practice test, each hints at the other. This is minor, and m2 is outside this review.

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **Current law says more than the manual in two places.** The content keeps the manual's answers in both cases.
   - **Headlights.** MGL c.85 §15 also requires lights whenever visibility drops below 500 feet or the wipers are needed. m7-16 is keyed to the manual, and its distractors stay wrong. You may want one sentence on the visibility rule in its explanation. I left it out because the app teaches what the manual says.
   - **Helmets.** MGL c.90 §7 exempts riders 18 or older in a properly permitted public parade, and the manual's "all passengers" omits this. The exemption predates the 2015 manual: a 2013 bill, [H.3095](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/188/H3095.Html), would have struck it. I see no reason to mention it to a learner.
2. **m7-09 is easy by elimination.** All three distractors (turn signals, engine overheating, horn) are mechanical or electrical failures, and the key is the only handling effect. More plausible handling distractors collide with the manual:
   - p.49 blames wobble on "improper loading" and "improper weight distribution".
   - p.33 says rear-mounted loads "affect braking".
   - MSF training, and p.12's "properly aimed headlight", make headlight aim arguable, which is why the interrupted pass removed it.

   I kept the safe set. You may prefer a harder question that still has only one defensible answer.
