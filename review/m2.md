## Changes

### Interrupted-pass edits, verified and kept

- **m2-01**: The stem changed from "what color light must the front of a motorcycle be fitted with?" to ask about the headlight. The manual's rule is specifically about the headlight (and, separately, a sidecar's forward light), so naming it removes any argument about other front lights. p.12: "The front of the motorcycle must be fitted with a white, properly aimed headlight."
- **m2-02**: The old distractors ("A red taillight only", etc.) were replaced with options that match the key's shape and differ from it in color or by making the plate light optional. The explanation was paraphrased; the old one copied a 10-word run. p.12: "The rear of the motorcycle must be fitted with a red tail light, a stoplight, and a white light to illuminate the license plate."
- **m2-03, m2-04, m2-05, m2-09, m2-14, m2-15, m2-16**: Distractors were lengthened or rewritten so the key no longer stands out by length. Before, the key was 2.04× the longest distractor in m2-04, 1.52× in m2-05, 1.57× in m2-09, 1.70× in m2-14 and 1.78× in m2-15 and m2-16. The m2-15 key was also shortened to "…one at a time; each should feel firm and hold the bike". I confirmed each new distractor is wrong under the manual:
  - p.12 requires "a secure seat and separate footpegs" for passengers and says nothing about backrests or handles; p.32 has passengers "Hold tightly to the rider's waist or hips".
  - p.13: "Helmet use for motorcycle riders and passengers is mandatory under Massachusetts law."
  - p.13: "Prior to purchasing a used motorcycle helmet, first ensure that the helmet is produced by a manufacturer or distributor that will re-inspect the helmet for damage."
  - p.14: "Soles should be made of hard, durable, slip-resistant material. Keep heels short… Tuck laces in…"
  - p.15: "Try the front and rear brake one at a time."
  - p.15: "All controls react a little differently."
- **m2-08**: The distractor "Open-face and half-shell" became "Fiberglass and polycarbonate". "Open-face" is a common name for the three-quarter style, so a learner could read the old pair as half right. The new pair is plainly not a helmet type. p.13: "two types of motorcycle helmets… the three-quarter and full faced styled helmets."
- **m2-10**: Distractors became "A tall windshield that reaches eye level", "Well-fitted, shatter-resistant goggles" and "Prescription eyeglasses with side shields". Goggles are the tempting choice, but p.14 rules them out: "Goggles protect your eyes, though they will not provide protection like a faceshield does." The explanation was paraphrased; the old one copied a 10-word run. p.13: "it is recommended that you wear a full faceshield to protect the eyes and face."
- **m2-11**: The stem gained "For protecting your eyes and face".
  - The distractor "A windshield replaces the need for goggles" was replaced because it is arguably true as a matter of law. p.9: "operators must wear eyeglasses, goggles, or a protective face shield, unless the motorcycle has a windshield or screen."
  - Its replacements are both contradicted by p.14: "A windshield protects your eyes just as well as goggles do", and "Most windshields will keep the wind out of a rider's eyes" (p.14: "Most windshields, eyeglasses, and sunglasses will not protect your eyes from the wind").
  - The explanation now separates the safety advice from the legal rule. I refined its wording; see below.
- **m2-12**: The distractor "When it is raining in daylight" became "When riding through heavy daytime city traffic". An overcast, rainy day could arguably count as a time "where little light is available" (p.14).
- **m2-13**: Distractors were rewritten so each one contradicts an item on the p.14 list. The list says protection must "Permit air to pass through to reduce fogging" and "Permit enough room for eyeglasses or sunglasses, if needed", and it never mentions tint. The explanation now also mentions room for glasses.
- **Bullet 6**: "Every helmet" became "Every motorcycle helmet". The manual's rule is about motorcycle helmets ("All motorcycle helmets sold in the United States are required to meet Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 218", p.13). The change also broke a 9-word copied run.
- **Bullet 7**: The defect list was reordered to break an 8-word copied run. The meaning is unchanged (p.13: "cracks, loose padding, frayed straps, or exposed metal").
- **Bullet 8**: "a windshield is never a substitute for either" clashed with the p.9 law. It now separates safety advice (p.14) from the legal exception (p.9). I refined its wording; see below.
- **Bullet 9**: Rewritten to break a 10-word copied run of p.14's "Tinted eye protection should not be worn at night or any other time where little light is available."

### New in this pass

- **m2-03 explanation**: Added that the shoulder rule is the legal limit, and that p.16 separately advises hands level with or below the elbows. That advice is tested in m3-04, and the two are easy to mix up on the real test. p.12: "Motorcycle handlebars must not rise above an operator's shoulders when properly seated on the motorcycle." p.16: "Adjust the handlebars so your hands are even with or below your elbows."
- **m2-06 explanation**: Removed "The manual cites NHTSA data". The manual's "According to NHTSA" introduces the lives-saved sentence, and the 67% sentence after it has no source of its own. Added the manual's qualifier. p.13: "Although a motorcycle helmet cannot prevent many other types of injuries, it is effective sixty-seven percent (67%) of the time in preventing brain injury."
- **m2-07 explanation**: The old explanation said "all helmets sold in the U.S." I changed it to "every motorcycle helmet", the same overstatement fixed in bullet 6. I also added why VESC-8 is wrong. It is the most tempting distractor because it appears in the manual, but for faceshields. p.13: "All motorcycle helmets sold in the United States are required to meet Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 218" and "A plastic shatter-resistant faceshield (VESC-8)".
- **m2-11 explanation and bullet 8**: The interrupted pass wrote "eye protection is not required on a bike that has a windshield or screen". I changed it to say the law "lets an operator skip eye protection", because p.9's rule covers operators only: "When operating a motorcycle, operators must wear eyeglasses, goggles, or a protective face shield, unless the motorcycle has a windshield or screen."
- **m2-16**: I replaced the distractor "Ride at your normal pace from the start so you learn how it handles as quickly as possible" with "Do your usual checks, then ride at your normal pace and following distance".
  - The problem: after the interrupted pass, every distractor ended in a "since…" or "so you…" excuse. The key was the only option without one and the only multi-step instruction.
  - The new distractor is multi-step and starts with correct advice. It is wrong on pace and on room to maneuver or stop.
  - The explanation now says why normal pace is wrong. p.15: "Ride very cautiously until you become familiar with the manner in which the motorcycle handles. For instance, accelerate gently, take turns more slowly, and leave yourself additional room for either maneuvering or stopping."
- **Bullet 2**: "Massachusetts requires that required safety equipment… be in good working order" now reads that the law sets minimum standards and the operator must keep all safety equipment in good working order. p.12: "Massachusetts law requires motorcycles to meet minimum safety equipment standards. A motorcycle operator must ensure that all safety equipment items are in good working order."
- **Bullet 4**: The old sentence joined the passenger rule and the handlebar rule with "and", so the handlebar limit could read as applying only to bikes set up for passengers. It also left out the operator's seat. It is now two sentences with "On any bike". p.12: "The motorcycle must be equipped with a secure seat for the operator. Should the motorcycle be equipped for carrying passengers, it must have a secure seat and separate footpegs. Motorcycle handlebars must not rise above an operator's shoulders when properly seated on the motorcycle."
- **New bullet 11**: Nothing in the section covered p.14's clothing-for-weather advice, so I added a bullet. p.14:
  - "It is recommended that riders wear jackets even in warm weather to prevent dehydration."
  - "In cold or wet weather, your clothes should keep you warm and dry"
  - "You cannot control a motorcycle well if you are numb from the cold. Riding for long periods in cold weather can cause severe chill, fatigue, and even hypothermia."

Specific confirmations requested in the brief:
- **Controls diagram (p.15)**: No question or bullet says where a control is. Bullets 12–13 mention checking controls, not their locations. For reference, I read the diagram, which is drawn from the rider's seat:
  - Left grip: clutch lever, horn button, turn-signal switch, light switch (high/low) and choke (varies).
  - Left foot: gear-change lever.
  - Right grip: throttle, front brake lever, engine cut-off switch and electric start button.
  - Right foot: rear brake pedal, plus the kick starter if equipped.
  - Center: ignition key, speedometer/odometer on the left, tachometer on the right, and the fuel supply valve (if equipped) on the left below the tank.
- **Helmet and eye-protection stems**: None overstates the manual.
  - m2-07 asks p.13's rule about helmets sold in the U.S. It does not say "Massachusetts requires".
  - m2-05 asks who must wear a helmet, which p.13 answers.
  - m2-10 to m2-13 ask about the manual's recommendations, not legal requirements. m2-11's stem is framed as protection, so p.9's legal exception does not make its key wrong.
- **Current law** (checked, not added to content):
  - The text of MGL c.90 §7 published by openjurist requires helmets for operators and passengers, and eye protection for operators of a motorcycle "not equipped with a windshield or screen" ([openjurist](https://openjurist.org/state-code/ma/90/90-7)).
  - 540 CMR 22.08 requires helmets that meet FMVSS No. 218 ([Justia](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/540-cmr/title-540-cmr-22-00/section-22-08)).
  - The 540 CMR 4.06 inspection criteria ([Justia](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/540-cmr/title-540-cmr-4-00/section-4-06)) reject handlebars or grips "higher than operator's shoulder level when properly seated". They also require footrests for each designated seating position, a firmly secured seat for each rider, white headlamps, a red tail light, a red stop lamp and a white plate light. So the p.12 equipment rules match current regulations.
  - MGL c.90 §7J leaves handlebar rules to the Registrar ([Justia, 2006 ed.](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/2006/gl-pt1-toc/90-7j.html)). openjurist.org/state-code/ma/90/90-7j was blocked when I tried it, so I used Justia instead.
  - H.3622 (2025–26), which would make helmets optional, was sent to a study order on 2/25/2026 ([malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H3622)). The universal helmet law the manual describes is still in force.

  I found no conflict between this section and current law.
