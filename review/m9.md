# m9 review: Motorcycle Laws, Turns & Impairment (printed pp. 48, 51–64)

Independent check of all 26 questions and all 15 summary bullets against the 2015 MA RMV Motorcycle Manual: printed p.48 ("Lane Use & Restrictions" / "Using Lanes" only) and pp.51–64 (PDF 55 and 58–71). Each page was read as an image (for layout, tables and captions) and as extracted text (for exact wording). All 26 keyed answers are directly supported by the manual. Every `page` value was already correct, no answer index changed, and no question was replaced. An earlier, interrupted pass had left unverified edits to four questions and two summary bullets, one of which it split in two. I checked each edit, kept all of them, and refined one (m9-01's fourth distractor). A script compared every explanation and summary bullet with the full manual text: none shares a run of 7+ words with it. `volatile` is true for exactly the three suspension-length questions (m9-24, 25, 26). The other 23 test rules, distances or BAC thresholds, so they stay `false`. Six questions now differ from the committed file: m9-01, 04, 12, 14, 23 and 24.

## Changes

Edits from the earlier pass, verified and kept:

- **m9-01** (leaving the right lane). The committed key was the only option listing several conditions, and its distractors were short single conditions. The earlier pass rewrote two distractors:
  - "When you are riding in a group" became "When riding in a group, at night, or with a passenger". **Kept.** None of these is a reason to leave the right lane. The group- and night-riding pages (pp.33–35) cover formation, speed, headlights and following distance only.
  - "Whenever it is dark out" became "When the pavement is wet or you are nearing a blind curve". **Refined** to "When you reach a curve and cannot see the road ahead". The wet-pavement half could be loosely argued from p.28 ("Adjusting lane position and cycle speed may be necessary and prudent when surfaces are slippery"). The blind-curve case is contradicted outright on the key's own page.

  I also added a sentence to the explanation: at a curve you can't see around, the manual says keep right and slow down. Support, p.48: "use the right lane for riding unless... You are passing another vehicle. You are making a left turn. The right lane is blocked." and "If you come to a curve in the road and cannot see ahead, keep to the right and slow down." Key unchanged.
- **m9-12** (four-way stop, simultaneous arrival). The earlier pass replaced the distractor "Whichever driver waves the other through" with "Whichever driver makes eye contact first". The explanation now says eye contact helps read intentions but doesn't decide who goes first. **Kept.** The old distractor was arguably defensible: p.54 says right-of-way "is something you give, not take" and that you "should always give the right of way" when another driver doesn't follow the rules, so a driver who waves you through is giving it to you. The new one uses the same page's eye-contact advice, which is only for judging intentions. Support, p.55: you yield to "A vehicle on your immediate right that has stopped at the intersection at the same time as you"; "You should try to make eye contact with the drivers of other vehicles at the intersection to better judge their intentions and avoid accidents." Key unchanged.
- **m9-23** (over-the-counter medicine). The earlier pass lengthened the three distractors, for example "They matter only if you have also been drinking" became "They affect your riding only if you have also been drinking alcohol". **Kept.** Before, the key was the longest option (66 characters against at most 58); now the distractors run 64–69. All three are still contradicted by p.60: "Illegal drugs, prescription medications, and over-the-counter (OTC) remedies all decrease the operator's reaction time, vision, and motor skills." Key unchanged.
- **m9-24 explanation** (first-OUI suspension). The earlier pass replaced "a court may let you shorten the suspension" with the actual reduced lengths. **Kept.** It matches the p.61 table, "For your first offense, the court may allow you to complete an alcohol education course to reduce your license suspension period.", which lists "Over Age 21, 45–90 days" and "Under Age 21, 210 days". The 1-year, 45–90-day and 210-day figures match mass.gov's current OUI pages. Key unchanged.
- **Summary bullet 8** (school buses). The earlier pass changed "the manual lists a $250 fine and license suspension for a first violation" to "the manual says a first violation can bring a $250 fine and license suspension". **Kept.** This restores the manual's "can"; p.56: "A first violation of this law can result in license suspension and a fine of $250." See judgment call 3.
- **Summary bullets 14–15** (penalties). The old single bullet gave the first-OUI suspension as a flat 1 year and left out the alcohol-education reduction. That changes what a first offender actually faces. The earlier pass split it in two. It added the 2½-year maximum prison term and the 45–90-day and 210-day reductions, and it changed "adults ... in a year" to "riders 18 and older ... within any one-year period". **Kept.** Support:
  - p.61: the OUI table.
  - p.62: "Three speeding violations/ responsible findings within any 1-year period" means 30 days.
  - p.63: speeding, "90 days— first offense".

  See judgment call 4 for the drug-conviction clause.

My edits:

- **m9-04** (restricted lanes). The key, "Only when preparing to make a turn", was the only restrictive option, and all three distractors began "Whenever…". A learner could pick the key on its form alone. I changed the distractors to begin "Only when…" and kept their content. I also rewrote the explanation to attribute the rule to the manual (see judgment call 2) and to say why the distractors fail. Support, p.51: "You must not ride in lanes posted as restricted, except when preparing for a turn." Key unchanged.
- **m9-14 explanation** (school bus, opposite direction). "Traffic in both directions must stop" stated the rule without its exception. It now reads "On an undivided road like this one, traffic in both directions must stop". Support, p.56: "No matter which side of the road you are traveling on … you must stop." and "The only exception to this law is if a school bus has stopped on the other side of a divided highway with a barrier between travel directions." Key unchanged.
- **Summary bullet 2** (using lanes). Added the blind-curve rule, which m9-01 now tests through a distractor. p.48: "If you come to a curve in the road and cannot see ahead, keep to the right and slow down."
- **Summary bullet 5** (turns). "turn right on red only after a full stop and yielding unless a sign forbids it" could be read as "unless a sign forbids it, you needn't stop". It now reads "turn right on red only after a full stop and after yielding, and never where NO TURN ON RED is posted". p.53: "After coming to a complete stop at a red traffic light, you are allowed to turn right on red after giving the right-of-way to pedestrians and other vehicles, unless a NO TURN ON RED sign is posted."
- **Summary bullet 6** (pedestrians). "come to a full stop for a blind pedestrian" left out the duty to stay stopped. Added "and stay stopped until they have crossed". p.54: "Remain stopped until the person has safely crossed."
- **Summary bullet 10** (parking). The bullet opened with "In business or residential districts", which grammatically limited the 20-foot intersection and 10-foot hydrant rules to those districts. The manual applies them everywhere: "In Massachusetts, you may not park your vehicle in certain places, including: … Within 20 feet of an intersection … Within 10 feet of a fire hydrant or fire lane". I reordered the bullet so the distance rules come first and apply generally, and only the 12-inch curb rule is limited to business or residential districts ("If you are parking in a business or residential district, you must position your vehicle within 12 inches of the curb, except where angled parking is allowed."). Also added "or fire lane". All p.58.

Classic test material, checked figure by figure:
- **Right-of-way.** At an uncontrolled intersection, yield to any vehicle "that has entered the intersection from your right or is approaching from your right" (m9-11, p.55). At a rotary, yield "to any vehicles already in the rotary" (m9-13, p.56).
- **School buses.** Traffic going either way must stop and remain stopped "until the lights stop flashing or the stop sign folds back". The only exception is a bus on the far side of a barrier-divided highway (m9-14, m9-15, p.56).
- **Implied consent.** "Police officers are required to immediately seize your operator's license if you register an illegal BAC or if you refuse a breathalyzer or blood test." The notice becomes "effective immediately" (m9-22, p.60). `volatile` stays false: mass.gov's current page still says the officer will "Take custody of your learner's permit/driver's license" and that "There is no notification period."
- **BAC.** 0.08% for riders 21 and older, 0.02% under 21. A 12 oz beer, 4 oz of wine or 1 oz of 80-proof liquor each holds about ½ oz of alcohol and adds 0.02% (m9-19, 20, 21, p.60).
- **Penalties.** m9-24 (1 year, p.61), m9-25 (30 days, p.62) and m9-26 (90 days, p.63; p.64 gives the same for license holders) match the manual and, per mass.gov today, current law. All three are `volatile: true`. m9-26 is the only junior-operator question, within the limit of three.

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **Two abreast vs. never riding alongside.** The manual says three things about riding side by side:
   - p.51: "Ride no more than two abreast" (m9-02).
   - p.27: "Motorcycle riders are prohibited from riding alongside motor vehicles within a marked travel lane" (m5-15 keys "No, it is prohibited").
   - p.34: "Riders should avoid 'pairing up' while traveling. Operating directly alongside another motorcycle is a dangerous practice…"

   The likely reading is that two abreast is the legal limit, pairing up is discouraged, and sharing a lane with other vehicles is barred. The manual never reconciles these. m9-02's explanation already points to the staggered-formation advice, and m7's working copy now says "avoid pairing up side by side", which agrees with m9-02. A human may still want a sentence linking m5-15 and m9-02, so a learner doesn't read p.27's "motor vehicles" as including a second motorcycle. I didn't add one, because saying which vehicles p.27 means would be my interpretation, not the manual's.
2. **Restricted lanes vs. HOV lanes (m9-04).** The manual bars any lane "posted as restricted, except when preparing for a turn", and p.47 names HOV lanes as a lane restriction. But mass.gov's page for the Southeast Expressway (I-93) HOV lane says it is "Open to buses and motorcycles". The question keeps the manual's answer, and the explanation now attributes the rule to the manual. "Posted as restricted" can also be read as restricted against you, which would remove the conflict for lanes posted open to motorcycles. A human may want an in-app note for real-world riding.
3. **School-bus penalty (summary bullet 8).** The manual says a first violation "can result in license suspension and a fine of $250". The current statute, MGL c.90 §14, sets "a fine of not less than $250" for a first offense, and revocation applies only from a second offense on. The bullet attributes the claim to the manual, and no question tests the penalty. Decide whether to keep or trim the penalty clause.
4. **Drug-conviction license loss (summary bullet 14).** p.60 says any drug conviction costs your license for 1–5 years, even when no vehicle was involved. A law signed March 30, 2016 repealed most of those automatic suspensions. It kept them for serious trafficking and for driving under the influence of drugs (WBUR). The bullet attributes the rule to the manual, and no question tests it. Keep it, since a manual-based test could still ask, or trim it.
5. **m9-14's stem hints at m9-15's key.** m9-14 says "undivided two-lane road", which signals that divided roads are treated differently, and that is exactly what m9-15 tests. I kept "undivided" because without it the barrier exception could apply and the stem would be ambiguous. In a practice test that draws both questions, the hint is mild.
