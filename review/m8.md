## Changes

- **m8-07** (steady yellow). The stem, options and key were correct, and the explanation was accurate and paraphrased. The gap: the most tempting distractor, "It is only a warning, so you may proceed with caution", is what a flashing yellow means, and the explanation never said so. The m8-08 explanation already draws the reverse contrast. The brief flagged this pair as easy to confuse, so I added one sentence: "Do not confuse it with a flashing yellow, which is only a warning to proceed with caution." Nothing else changed. Support, p.38: "A steady yellow light means the traffic signal is changing from green to red." and "A flashing yellow light is a warning."
- **m8-17** (4-WAY / ALL WAY stop). I replaced the distractor "The vehicle on the wider road". The manual's right-of-way rules on p.56 make drivers on a one- or two-lane road yield to vehicles on a divided highway or a road with three or more lanes, so a learner could argue the old distractor is partly right. The new one, "The largest vehicle, since it needs the most room", has no support anywhere in the manual. Answer unchanged. Support, p.40: "The first vehicle in the intersection of a four-way stop has the right of way."
- **m8-18** (orange diamond sign). The correct option was the only one with a reason clause, and it was the longest (61 characters against at most 52, 1.17×). I added parallel clauses to two distractors: "A school zone is just ahead, so watch for children" and "A recreational or scenic area is ahead, so watch for its entrance". Answer unchanged. Support: p.40, "ORANGE - construction or maintenance warning"; p.42, "you should take extra care when orange signs are posted."
- **m8-26** (painted white diamond). The correct option was clearly the longest (57 characters against at most 46, 1.24×). I lengthened two distractors: "A pedestrian crosswalk is just ahead, so be ready to stop" and "A railroad crossing is ahead, so slow down and look for trains". Both stay wrong under the manual: a crosswalk is a pair of white lines (p.48), and railroad crossings are announced by a round sign (p.45). Answer unchanged. ### Notes on the requested checks

- **m8-02, m8-03, m8-04** (speed figures). p.36 says "In Massachusetts, the minimum speed on interstate and limited access highways is 45 mph", and that posted limits there range "from 50 to 65 mph". p.37 says "Unless posted otherwise, your speed would not be considered reasonable and proper if you were riding over...", followed by 20, 30, 40 and 50 mph. The "not considered reasonable and proper" framing in m8-03 and m8-04 matches the manual. All three are correctly `volatile: true`. The m8-04 stem repeats the manual's category name, "in a thickly settled or business district" (7 words). That is a legal term, not prose, and it sits in the stem rather than the explanation, so I left it.
- **m8-13** (red and yellow together). I kept it. It is answerable from the manual alone: p.39 says "You must yield to pedestrians if your traffic signal is red or if it is red and yellow", which supports the key directly. The manual rules out each distractor:
  - "Pedestrians must wait while your signal shows yellow": the same p.39 law requires yielding to them.
  - "Continue without yielding": same law.
  - "Turn right without stopping": a right on red requires a complete stop first (p.37).

  See judgment call 1.
- **m8-16, m8-18, m8-19, m8-20** (sign colors and shapes). Checked against the color key on p.40 and the shape chart on p.42:
  - brown means recreational, historic or scenic site
  - orange means construction or maintenance
  - a sideways triangle pointing right means "No Passing Zone"
  - the five-sided, house-shaped outline means "School Zone"

  The yellow color in the m8-19 stem matches the "You may not cross the yellow line to pass" sign in the p.43 warning chart. All four describe signs in words only.
- **m8-07 / m8-08** (steady vs flashing yellow). Each stands on its own. The steady-yellow distractor "It is only a warning…" is the flashing meaning. The flashing-yellow distractors describe a steady yellow ("about to change from green to red"), a flashing red ("complete stop, then go") and a steady red ("wait until the light turns green"). Neither stem can be read as the other.
- **m8-17 vs p.55.** The fuller four-way-stop rules on p.55 (yield to a vehicle that has already stopped, and to the vehicle on your immediate right if you arrive together) are tested in m9-12. They agree with the p.40 rule keyed in m8-17, and no m8-17 option conflicts with them.
- **Summary bullets.** All 14 are accurate for pp.36–48 and paraphrased, with no copied run of 7 or more words. The color and shape bullets match the charts on pp.40 and 42, and the railroad bullet matches p.45 ("at least 15 feet before the light post or gate").

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **m8-13: keep or replace?** I kept it because the p.39 manual line supports the key and nothing else. Two drawbacks:
   - The manual never explains what a red-and-yellow display is, so the question rests on recalling one bullet.
   - The answer, yield to pedestrians, is also true at a steady red alone, so it overlaps m8-15.

   In Massachusetts practice, red plus yellow marks an exclusive pedestrian phase. Lowell's pedestrian ordinance, for example, treats it as a "go" indication for pedestrians. Under 720 CMR 9.06, a lit red lens means stop outside the intersection, and a lit yellow lens means waiting drivers shall not proceed. Read together, these suggest vehicles must stay stopped, with no right on red, which is stricter than "yield". The manual does not say this. The keyed answer is incomplete rather than wrong, but its wording "as you must at a red light" could suggest a right on red after yielding is fine during this phase. If you replace it, one option from this section is the p.38 flashing-red rule: with no stop line or crosswalk, stop where you can see both directions without entering the intersection.
2. **m8-02 (45 mph minimum).** The answer matches the manual, but outside sources dispute it. A third-party list of claimed errors in MA manuals (mit.edu/~jfc) says there is no general minimum on limited-access highways, only specific ones such as 40 mph on the Mass Pike. mass.gov's speed-limit law page quotes the current Driver's Manual as giving a 40 mph Turnpike minimum and lists no general 45 mph minimum. The stem already says "According to the manual" and the question is flagged volatile. Consider whether its caution note should point learners to mass.gov specifically for this one.
3. **m8-04 (30 mph thickly settled).** Legislation passed in 2016 (MGL c.90 §17C) lets cities and towns that opt in lower this default to 25 mph, and many have, including Boston (opted in January 2017). The question is keyed to the manual's 30 mph and flagged volatile, which is right for a test drawn from this manual. A human may want the explanation to mention the local 25 mph option. I left it out because the app teaches what the manual says.
