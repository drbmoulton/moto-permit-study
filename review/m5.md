# m5 review: Space Management (printed pp. 25–27)

Independent check of all 18 questions and all 12 summary bullets against the 2015 MA RMV Motorcycle Manual, printed pp. 25–27 (PDF 32–34), from "Keeping Your Distance" up to "Dangerous Surfaces". I read each page as an image, with the Passing, Being passed, Intersections, Parked vehicles and Merging traffic diagrams re-rendered at 200 dpi, and as extracted text. I checked every distractor against the whole manual, not just these pages, and against what other sections of the app already teach. Every keyed answer is supported by the manual. No answer index changed, no question was replaced, every `page` value was already correct, and every `volatile` flag is correctly `false`: following distances in seconds are not fees, fines, speed figures or procedures. 10 questions and 4 summary bullets changed. The most serious problem was m5-04, where a distractor is advice the manual itself gives on p.20. Before this pass the build warned that the key was clearly the longest option in 5 of 18 questions, and the key was strictly the longest in 9 of 18. Now none is above 1.3×, and the key is strictly the longest in 3 of 18, by at most 1.11×. No explanation or summary bullet shares a run of 7+ words with the manual; the longest is 6.

## Changes

- **m5-04** (tailgater). The distractor "Tap your brakes repeatedly to warn the driver" was arguably correct. p.20 says "It is especially important to flash your brake lights if being followed too closely ("tailgated".)", and m4-08 teaches exactly that. The key was also a giveaway at 98 characters against 45 (2.18×). New options: key "Open up space ahead and change lanes to let it pass", and distractors "Speed up to pull away from the tailgating vehicle", "Slow to a stop at the edge of the lane so it can go by" and "Keep your speed and lane position, and ignore it". The stopping option is contradicted by p.24: "If the vehicle following behind is dangerously close, it may be best for the rider to continue moving forward to avoid serious injury." It shares 6 words with the p.56 rotary rule ("stop at the edge of the rotary and wait"), which has nothing to do with tailgaters. The explanation now gives the manual's reason, mentions the p.20 brake-light tip so she doesn't conclude it is wrong, and says why speeding up and stopping are wrong. Key unchanged. Support, p.25: "increase the stopping distance between the vehicles ahead to ensure that a proper space cushion exists in the event of an emergency or traffic hazard. If the opportunity presents itself, change vehicle lanes allowing the motorist's vehicle to safely pass." The manual gives no figure for this case, and nothing in m5 claims one.
- **m5-03** (four-second rule). The key was 2.24× the longest distractor (74 against 33). Two distractors named conditions the manual elsewhere ties to more space, and only the word "Only" made them wrong:
  - "Only when riding with a passenger": p.32 says "Riders should incorporate a larger cushion of space when stopping or slowing the cycle."
  - "Only at night": p.35 recommends "reducing cycle speed, maximizing headlight usage, and increasing following distance."

  I replaced all three distractors with conditions the manual never links to following distance: "Whenever you are riding slower than 30 mph", "On familiar, uncrowded roads with dry pavement" and "Whenever you have less than a year of riding experience". The key is tightened to "On unfamiliar, congested, or slippery roads". The stem now reads "When does the manual recommend…", which attributes the figure (see judgment call 1). The explanation adds that "unfamiliar" means the area, not the rider's experience, which is the most tempting distractor. Support, p.25: "If the area of travel is unfamiliar, heavily congested, or the pavement is slippery, it is recommended that riders subscribe to a "four-second" (or more) distance rule."
- **m5-15** (lane sharing). The old stem was "In Massachusetts, may a motorcycle ride alongside another motor vehicle within the same marked travel lane?" It had two problems:
  1. It stated a legal rule as Massachusetts law, but the manual names no state or statute at that point. I reframed it as "According to the manual…".
  2. "Another motor vehicle" includes motorcycles. p.51 says "Ride no more than two abreast", which m9-02 teaches, so a careful learner could defend "Yes, if both drivers agree" for two motorcycles. The stem now asks about "a car".

  I reworded one distractor to "Yes, if you keep to the right edge of the lane" to match "you". The old explanation joined two separate manual statements with an unsupported "because". The new one states both and adds that riding beside another motorcycle falls under the special rules (p.51). Key unchanged. Support, p.27: "All motor vehicles are permitted to use the full lane of travel to ensure safe operation. Motorcycle riders are prohibited from riding alongside motor vehicles within a marked travel lane." See judgment calls 2 and 3 for the statute.
- **m5-10** (why ride in the center when passed). The stem "Why does riding in the center of the lane help when you are being passed…" gave away m5-09's answer, so it is now "Why does the manual's recommended lane position help when a larger vehicle is passing you?". The key was also 1.88× the longest distractor (90 against 48). I shortened it to "It guards against sideswipes, debris, and wind gusts" and reworded the distractors to similar lengths, keeping their ideas. The explanation now says why "It keeps your tires out of the oily strip" is wrong. p.28: "Center lane travel may be hazardous due to the accumulation of oil left on the surface by passing motor vehicles." Key unchanged. Support, p.26: "This added cushion of space prevents the rider from being sideswiped by passing vehicles or inadvertently struck by road objects and debris. Traveling in the center of the travel lane further protects the rider from wind shear or gusts created by larger vehicles…"
- **m5-14** (parked-vehicle hazard). The key was 1.58× the longest distractor (57 against 36). It also blurred two hazards: drivers getting out of their cars, and pedestrians stepping between cars. The new key is "A pedestrian stepping out between parked cars". New distractors:
  - "Wind blast from trucks parked along the curb" (lengthened).
  - "Glare off the windshields of parked cars", replacing "Low-hanging tree branches".
  - "Exhaust fumes from cars idling at the curb", replacing "Grooved pavement near parking meters". Grooves appear on this same page, under Dangerous Surfaces ("grooves or gratings can significantly affect motorcycle traction and balance", p.27), which invited confusion.

  Glare, fumes and exhaust appear nowhere in the manual. Support, p.27: "to avoid problems caused by either operators exiting their vehicles or pedestrians stepping between vehicles."
- **m5-17** (car alongside). The key was 1.32× the longest distractor (62 against 47). It is now "Adjust your speed to open a gap between you". The old explanation, "may suddenly change direction into you", narrowed the manual's point. It now says the car may "change direction without warning and force you into danger", and attributes the advice to the manual. Support, p.27: "vehicles traveling in the adjacent lane may unexpectedly change direction forcing the rider into a potentially dangerous situation. Adjust cycle speed until a proper and adequate cushion of space has been established between vehicles."
- **m5-06** (before signaling to pass). Two distractors were defensible:
  - "Sound your horn": p.21 links the horn to passing ("Horn use is also recommended when the ability to safely pass a motor vehicle entering your lane of travel is in question").
  - "Downshift two gears": sound real-world practice, and the stem does not limit itself to the manual.

  I replaced them with "Use your mirrors only and skip the head check" and "Move to the right portion of your lane". The first contradicts the mirrors-plus-head-check rule and the second the left-portion setup. "Close the gap with the vehicle ahead" became "Close the gap to the vehicle you plan to pass". The key is now 0.98× the longest distractor, down from 1.22×. The explanation now says the manual asks for both checks. Support, p.26: "When there is sufficient space, use vehicle mirrors and a quick "head-check" to gauge traffic conditions behind and to the sides of the motorcycle before signaling and accelerating to the desired lane of travel."
- **m5-02** (failed two-second check). The stem opened "Using the two-second rule…" and quoted the count up to two, which gives away m5-01. It is now "You check your following distance against a fixed object and reach it before you finish the recommended count." The key was the longest option at 1.22×, so I shortened it from "…until you have more distance from the vehicle ahead" to "Slow down until there is enough space ahead". Support, p.25: "If you reach the object before you count two, you are following too closely. Slow down until you have put enough distance between you and the other vehicle."
- **m5-01** (normal following distance). I changed the weak distractor "Five seconds" to "Four seconds". The question now tests normal conditions (two seconds) against unfavorable ones (four or more), the classic confusion. Four is clearly wrong for normal conditions under p.25. Key unchanged.
- **m5-05** (passing setup). The explanation said the left-portion setup "lets you see ahead". The manual's only stated reason is "to increase rider visibility" (p.26), and the manual uses that phrase to mean being seen (pp.19, 20, 33). I removed the unsupported claim.
- **Summary bullets.**
  - **Bullet 1:** "best protection in a crash situation" became "best protection against serious injury or death in a crash". Space protects you by avoiding the crash, not during it. p.25: "space is the best protection against being seriously injured or killed in a motor vehicle accident."
  - **Bullet 6:** added the step "check for oncoming traffic and hazards". p.26: "Prior to activating your signals, check for on-coming traffic and potential road hazards."
  - **Bullet 10:** it said the left portion keeps you clear of cars "making U-turns". The manual only says to approach such cars cautiously, and the diagram shows the U-turning car sweeping across the whole road. It now says "approach cars pulling out or making U-turns with caution". p.27: "Cautiously approach vehicles pulling away from curbs or parking spaces. The potential for injury with vehicles making U-turns presents yet another danger to the rider."
  - **Bullet 11:** "Riding alongside another vehicle within a single marked lane is prohibited" had the same two-abreast conflict as m5-15. It now reads "The manual prohibits riding beside a car within one marked lane".

## Checked with no change

8 of 18 questions needed no change: m5-07, 08, 09, 11, 12, 13, 16, 18. The other 8 summary bullets (2–5, 7–9, 12) are accurate and paraphrased.

Specific confirmations:
- **Following-distance figures.** On p.25, two seconds is the baseline ("If you reach the object before you count two, you are following too closely"), and four seconds or more applies only when the area is unfamiliar, heavily congested, or slippery. Elsewhere in the manual:
  - p.34: a minimum two-second cushion between staggered riders in the same track (tested in m7-13).
  - p.35, night: "increasing following distance", with no figure.
  - p.32, passengers: a larger cushion when stopping or slowing, with no figure.
  - p.25, being tailgated: more space ahead, with no figure.

  No m5 question, explanation or bullet attaches a number to the cases that have none. "Scan 10 to 15 seconds ahead" (p.19) is a look-ahead distance, not a following distance, and is not used here.
- **m5-02 distractor "Flash your brake light at the vehicle ahead"** kept. The p.20 brake-light advice is about alerting drivers behind you. A brake light cannot signal the vehicle ahead, and the manual's remedy for following too closely is to slow down.
- **m5-05:** the shoulder distractor is wrong under p.52: "Never use a breakdown lane, the shoulder of a road, or a sidewalk for passing another vehicle."
- **m5-07** matches p.26 ("Quickly pass through the operator's blind spot"), p.21 ("get through the driver's blind spot as quickly as possible") and p.51.
- **m5-12:** "closest to the curb" is wrong under p.28, which says to stay away from the road's edge near intersections because dirt and gravel collect there. The lane-line distractors in m5-12, m5-16 and m5-17 are wrong under p.51: "Do not ride along pavement lines or between lanes of traffic."
- **m5-18:** the key is the longest option (79 against 71, 1.11×), which is under the threshold and not noticeably longer, so I left it. "Assume the driver sees you" stays wrong even though p.51 tells entering drivers to yield, because p.27 says outright not to assume the merging driver will recognize you.
- **Diagrams.** The pp.26–27 diagrams agree with the text: pass setup from the left portion behind the car, centered riders while being passed, a car turning across the rider's path at the intersection, a U-turning car leaving a parking space, and a car merging from the right.

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **Four seconds against MSF's three.** The MSF Motorcycle Operator Manual gives "a minimum of two seconds distance" normally and says to "open a three-second or more following distance" when the pavement is slippery or traffic is heavy. That wording is from the Kansas MSF edition, Rev. 2020 (ksrevenue.gov/pdf/mchdbk.pdf), and the Iowa manual developed with MSF matches it (publications.iowa.gov/35297/1/mcmanual.pdf). The learner passed the MSF Basic RiderCourse, so "three" is her most likely wrong answer. The content keeps the manual's four, m5-03's stem attributes it to the manual, and no MSF figure appears in the content. m5-03 tests the conditions, and "four" appears only in its stem, so she could answer it without memorizing the number. A human may want an in-app note, or a question that asks the number for unfavorable conditions with "Three seconds" as a distractor. m5-01 now uses "Four seconds" as a distractor.
2. **The manual contradicts itself on riding side by side.** The lane-sharing paragraph prohibits riding alongside "motor vehicles", a term that includes motorcycles (p.27). The special rules allow two abreast (p.51), and the group-riding section calls pairing up "a dangerous practice" (p.34). MGL c.89 §4A matches p.51: motorcyclists "shall not ride abreast of more than one other motorcycle" and "shall not pass any other motor vehicle within the same lane, except another motorcycle" (malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter89/Section4a; Justia, 2025 code). I resolved the conflict by asking about a car and adding a one-sentence pointer to the special rules in the explanation. The manual never reconciles the two passages itself, so a human may prefer to drop that sentence.
3. **"In Massachusetts" framing for m5-15.** The statute supports the substance for cars. §4A ("When any way has been divided into lanes…") requires a vehicle to stay within a single lane, and it bars a motorcycle from passing another motor vehicle within the same lane unless that vehicle is a motorcycle. It does not use the manual's words "riding alongside", so I attributed the rule to the manual rather than to state law. A human could restore the state framing with a citation if the app should state law. The current §4A text still matches the manual for cars, and the most recent attempt to loosen lane rules failed: H.3620 (2025), which would have let motorcycles use breakdown lanes in slow traffic, went to a study order on 2/25/2026 and was not enacted (malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H3620).
