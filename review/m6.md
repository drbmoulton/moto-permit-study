# m6 review: Hazards & Emergencies (printed pp. 27–31, 49–50)

Independent check of all 24 questions and all 14 summary bullets against the 2015 MA RMV Motorcycle Manual. Each page was read as an image (for layout, diagrams and captions) and as extracted text (for exact wording). All 24 keyed answers are directly supported by the manual. No question was replaced, no answer index changed, and every `page` value was already correct. No explanation or summary bullet shares a run of 7+ words with the manual. No correct option is noticeably longer than its distractors. Every `volatile` flag is correctly `false`: this section has no fees, fines, penalties, speed figures or procedures.

## Changes

- **m6-06**: Distractor "Change your path so you cross the tracks at a 90-degree angle" was arguably defensible. p.29's general obstacle advice lists railroad tracks among obstacles and says to approach an obstacle "at a ninety degree angle", and a learner could fairly cite it. Rewrote the distractor as "Change your path to cross at 90 degrees, since smaller angles are unsafe". The added reason contradicts the track paragraph directly ("able to safely cross railroad tracks at angles as sharp as forty-five degrees"), so the option is now plainly wrong. Added a sentence to the explanation saying why the track-specific passage overrides the general obstacle rule. Key unchanged (p.29).
- **m6-17**: The explanation did not say that the rearward weight shift is the manual's own recommendation, as the brief requires. Rewrote it to open with "The manual recommends moving your weight toward the rear…", supported by p.49: "it is recommended that the rider shift his or her weight to the rear of the motorcycle for added balance and stability." Key unchanged.
- **m6-18**: The explanation stated the manual's abstract rule, brake gradually with the tire that is still good, without connecting it to the answer. Rewrote it to say outright that with a flat rear tire the good tire is the front, so you use the front brake (p.49: "gradually apply the brake to the tire which has not failed"). Also changed distractor "Apply the rear brake firmly" to "Apply the rear brake gradually". The old "firmly" let a learner eliminate it on intensity alone; now "front vs. rear" is the only difference from the key, which tests the rule itself. Key unchanged.
- **m6-20**: Distractor "Hit the engine cut-off switch, then let the clutch lever out" was ambiguous. Letting the clutch out after the engine is already off is not clearly wrong, and the manual never mentions releasing the clutch. Rewrote it as "Use the engine cut-off switch without pulling in the clutch", which directly contradicts p.49 ("activate the engine cut-off switch while holding in the cycle's clutch"). Key unchanged.
- **Summary bullet 8 (quick stops)**: "release and gently reapply a locked front wheel" is imprecise, since you release and reapply the brake, not the wheel. Rewrote it as "release the front brake at once and reapply it gently if the front wheel locks" (p.31: "immediately release the front brake lever and gently reapply brake pressure").
- **Summary bullet 10 (animals)**: The bullet left out the manual's main instruction: make every effort to avoid hitting an animal. On its own, "don't swerve into another lane" could read as "don't try to avoid it". Added "Try hard not to hit an animal, but…" (p.31: "should make every effort to avoid hitting an animal").

## Checked with no change

20 of 24 questions needed no change: m6-01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 21, 22, 23, 24. The other 12 summary bullets are accurate and paraphrased.

Specific confirmations requested in the brief:
- m6-06 / m6-07 match pp.29–30 exactly. Railroad tracks: safe "at angles as sharp as forty-five degrees", and riders are "discouraged from altering their ordinary course" to cross at 90 degrees. Parallel trolley tracks and seams are "edge-traps": make "a quick and sharp turn", at angles "of at least forty-five degrees". The "Cross tracks" and "Parallel tracks" Correct/Incorrect diagrams agree.
- m6-11 matches p.31 word for word in substance: "immediately release the front brake lever and gently reapply brake pressure."
- m6-12 matches p.31: "provided the cycle is traveling in a straight line, keep it locked until the cycle has come to a complete stop." The stem states the straight-line condition.

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **m6-20's stem gives away m6-19's answer.** "You have twisted a stuck throttle back and forth, but it will not come free" tells the reader that twisting is the first step. In a section test, a learner who sees m6-20 first gets m6-19 for free. In a practice test both could be drawn. I left it alone because a two-step sequence question needs to state step 1. Possible fix: change m6-20's stem to something like "Your throttle is stuck and your first attempt to free it has failed."
2. **m6-17 and m6-18 teach this manual's tire-failure advice.** It differs from mainstream rider training (MSF), which generally says not to shift weight and to brake on the good tire only after the bike is under control. Per the brief, the questions keep the manual's answers, and the explanations now say these are the manual's recommendations. A human may want an in-app note that real-world training differs.
3. **m6-06's 45-degree advice versus the general 90-degree obstacle rule.** The manual says to meet obstacles, railroad tracks included, at 90 degrees, then gives the 45-degree track exception a few lines later. The question follows the more specific track passage, which is the reading most likely on the test. The explanation now addresses the conflict, but the conflict is in the manual itself.
4. **m6-15's distractor "Hold your line and make no attempt to avoid it"** is wrong per p.31 ("make every effort to avoid hitting an animal"). It stays wrong only while that sentence is read as overriding the no-swerve rule in heavy traffic. I left it as is because the key ("Avoid it if you can, but don't swerve into another lane") matches the full passage.
