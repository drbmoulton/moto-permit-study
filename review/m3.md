### My changes

- **m3-06 (explanation).** I checked the earlier pass's distractor "Feet dragging lightly along the ground to help balance in slow turns" against p.28. There the manual has riders cross unavoidable snow or ice "with feet skimming the surface for added balance". That exception is for ice with the bike "straight up", not for turns. p.16 says "Do not drag your feet along the ground", and the p.10 road test runs figure-eights "without your feet touching". So I kept the distractor. I added one sentence naming the ice exception so the explanation does not seem to contradict m6-03, which keys that exception. Key unchanged (p.16).
- **m3-12 (explanation).** The committed explanation copied 8 words ("and can provide at least three-quarters of"), and the earlier pass paraphrased it. I added "The manual says" because current MSF-based manuals give a different figure (judgment call 2). Key unchanged. p.18: "The front brake is more powerful and can provide at least three-quarters of your total stopping power."
- **m3-14 (explanation).** The old explanation said why rear-first is wrong but never stated the rule. It now opens with "Apply both brakes at once." p.18: "Apply both brakes at the same time. Many individuals believe that the rear brake should be applied first. That is not true." Key unchanged.
- **m3-16 (one distractor, explanation).** I kept two of the earlier pass's distractors. The third, "Only the rear brake should be used in a turn, since cornering uses up none of the traction", was wrong but incoherent: its reason does not support its claim. It also repeated the first distractor's idea that the front brake is off-limits.
  - I rewrote it as "Hard braking is safe while leaned, as long as you use only the rear brake". This tests the bullet's other point, that too much brake skids whichever brake you use. p.18: "A skid can occur if you apply too much brake"; "jamming down on the rear can cause the brakes to lock".
  - The explanation now covers this. The key is still 0.98× the longest distractor.
  - Key unchanged. p.18: "The front brake can be used in a turn, provided the proper technique is applied... less traction is available for stopping."
- **m3-18 (explanation).** The distractor "The front wheel may lift off the ground" is what p.19 says happens on a hill start: "If you release it too quickly, the front wheel may come off the ground, the engine may stop, or both." I added a sentence saying the manual ties front-wheel lift to the hill-start clutch, so she doesn't learn "front wheel lifts" as a wrong answer everywhere.
- **m3-19 (explanation).** The explanation now carries the manual's condition, which no question tests directly. p.18: "Do not attempt to upshift or downshift in a turn unless you can shift smoothly. A sudden change in power to the rear wheel can cause it to lock or spin."

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **"Slow, Look, Lean, Roll" vs her course's "Slow, Look, Press, Roll."** Current MSF-based state manuals call the third step "Press": Georgia ("Press forward on the handgrip in the direction of the turn", [source](https://dds.georgia.gov/book/export/html/33941)) and Utah ([source](https://dld.utah.gov/motorcycle-handbook-section-4-ride-within-your-abilities/)). This manual says "Lean", as does New York's older text ([source](https://dmv.ny.gov/book/export/html/14381)). m3-07's options are all orderings of the manual's four words, so she can't pick "Press" by mistake, and the explanation attributes the sequence to the manual. A one-line heads-up that the manual's word differs from her BRC may be worth adding in the app.
2. **Front-brake share.** The manual says "at least three-quarters". The Georgia and Utah MSF-based manuals say "70% or more of your total stopping power". m3-12 is keyed to the manual, and no option is 70%, so the figure she may remember from her course still points to the key. The explanation now attributes the figure to the manual.
3. **Coverage gaps on these pages.** Some p.16–19 facts could appear on the test without being practiced here:
   - Integrated braking systems: on some bikes the rear pedal applies both brakes, and the manual says to consult the owner's manual (p.18). No question or bullet covers this.
   - Releasing the clutch too fast on a hill can lift the front wheel or stall the engine (p.19). This now appears only in m3-18's explanation.
