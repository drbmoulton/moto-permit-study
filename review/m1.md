# m1 review: Licensing & the Road Test (printed pp. 2–11)

I checked all 15 questions and all 12 summary bullets against the 2015 MA RMV Motorcycle Manual, printed pp. 2–11 (PDF 9–18). I read every page as an image (for layout and captions) and as extracted text (for exact wording). I also searched the rest of the manual for anything that could make a distractor defensible.

- All 15 keyed answers are directly supported by the manual. No answer index changed, no question was replaced, and every `page` value was already correct.
- An earlier, interrupted pass had left unverified edits to 11 questions and 4 bullets. I checked each one against the manual: all are kept, three are refined (m1-07's explanation, bullets 8 and 11), and none are reverted.
- I checked the licensing facts against current mass.gov pages and Massachusetts statutes (sources at the end). Where they differ from the manual, the content keeps the manual's answer and says so.

## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **Road-test details that mass.gov has changed since 2015.**
   - The current road-test page says a road test on a trike or limited use vehicle gives a Class M license with a **"U"** restriction. p.10 says "J". Bullet 11 now attributes "J" to the manual, and no question tests the letter. Dropping the letter from the bullet is the other option.
   - The same page lists the road-test skills without "Riding up and down hills". Bullet 12 keeps the manual's list.
   - It also gives out-of-state insurance minimums of $25,000/$50,000 and $30,000, against p.9's $20,000/$40,000 and $5,000. Nothing in m1 uses these figures.
2. **Current law or practice qualifies three manual statements that m1 uses.**
   - **MREP no longer replaces the road test for minors.** mass.gov says you need no Class M road test after MREP "unless you are under 18 years of age". p.7 has no age exception, and bullet 7 follows the manual. The bullet is still right for an adult, and no question tests the point.
   - **Junior operator night hours.** MGL c.90 §8 lets a junior operator drive between 12:30 and 5:00 a.m. when "accompanied by a parent or legal guardian".
     - m1-07's key is unaffected, and its explanation now attributes the rule to the manual. Bullet 8 still states the hours without the exception.
     - Separately, mass.gov's JOL page gives a midnight–5:00 a.m. limit for learner's permit holders under 18. That is a different rule, absent from the manual, so seeing "midnight" there does not mean m1-07's key is wrong.
   - **Scooters on limited-access highways.** MGL c.90 §1E bars motorized scooters only from limited-access or express highways "where signs specifically prohibiting scooters or bicycles have been posted". Under the statute, m1-10's distractor "It may be ridden on limited-access highways in the right lane" could be true on an unsigned highway. Under the manual it is plainly wrong (p.5: "Do not drive on limited-access or express state highways."), and m1-10's explanation now attributes the ban to the manual.
3. **The m1-02 / m1-07 contrast pair.** m1-02's distractor "Only between 5:00 a.m. and 12:30 a.m." is the reverse of m1-07's key, and m1-07's explanation states m1-02's key (sunrise to sunset). Section tests shuffle the questions, so whichever comes first hints at the other. I kept both because the confusion is worth teaching, as the m8 review did with steady and flashing yellow. If that is unwanted: change m1-02's distractor to a time span tied to no rule, and drop the second sentence of m1-07's explanation.
4. **m1-13 duplicates m2-07.** Both questions key FMVSS No. 218, and since the earlier pass's VESC-8 edit, three of their four options are identical. I left m1-13 because it is sound and m2 is another reviewer's file. One way to make it test a different fact from p.9: "How can you tell a helmet meets the required standard?" Support, p.9: "Helmets meeting this standard will be labeled with stickers on the interior and exterior of the helmet."
