I read each page twice: as an image (layout, captions, and the hand-signal and lane-position figures at 300 dpi) and as extracted text (exact wording). An earlier interrupted pass had left unverified edits in this file. I checked each one against the manual and kept, refined or extended it, as noted below; none needed reverting. ## Judgment calls left for a human

1. **Which arm for hand signals (m4-09, summary 6).** The manual's figure doesn't show which arm, and the text doesn't say. Massachusetts law doesn't specify one either: M.G.L. c.90 §14B ([malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter90/Section14B)) gives the same three positions, "extended horizontally" for left, "extended upward" for right, "extended downward" to stop or slow.
   - Common rider training does use the left arm. A [Harley-Davidson guide](https://www.harley-davidson.com/us/en/content/expert-advice/motorcycle-hand-signals-group-riding.html) says the MSF signals are "executed with the left hand and arm". That guide also gives the MSF stop signal as the arm "straight down, palm facing back", where the manual's figure shows it angled down and out.
   - The app follows the figure and names no arm. If you want "left arm" back in the options, that source supports it, but the manual does not.
   - The statute requires hand signals only when the electric signals aren't working. The manual's advice to use both "routinely" goes beyond the law but doesn't conflict with it.
2. **m4-14 is a replacement, not a rewrite.** The brief prefers rewrites. I kept the earlier pass's replacement because the committed question was a near-copy of m5-07, and any version testing the same fact stays a duplicate. Another p.21 fact could be used instead, such as "decide early whether to pass or drop back", or parking at an angle so drivers can see the motorcycle.
3. **Coverage of pp.22–24.** With the question count fixed, several classic items appear only in the summary or not at all:
   - SPIDER beyond "D". Examples: Identify's three hazard groups (vehicles; animals and pedestrians; stationary objects such as potholes and guardrails), and Execute, which is riding within your skill.
   - Checking mirrors "every few seconds".
   - "Continue moving forward" when a follower is dangerously close.
   - Practicing with convex mirrors until you judge distance well.

   A human may want to swap a low-value item for one of these. m4-13 (three paths) or m4-07 (the "twice" figure) are the obvious candidates.
